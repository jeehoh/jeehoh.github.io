I recently came across Prathosh A.P.'s lecture on "Pedagogy in the Times of AI". In the lecture, he shares his concerns about recent advancements in AI (with a premise that we have already achieved an artificial general intelligence (AGI)).

But is this concern justified? Here are some of my thoughts:

- It feels like nobody understands what AGI actually looks like yet. Given that our definition of AI constantly changes with each new breakthrough, I wouldn't be surprised if it never gets defined.
- I do feel that a lot of the concerns are worrisome, but they are also higly theoretical (i.e., list of potential harms that AGI could bring). But I also feel like justifying AI policy and safety research based on theoretical concerns also misses the point. I.e., are there things that we could do _now_ about our _current_ problems so that we could avoid having _future_ problems?

For example, an AI doomerist might say: "Why bother studying CS? AI is going to program everything anyways in the future." But think carefully about this statement. It makes a lot of assumptions: "AI is already perfect", "human programmers are unnecessary in the future", etc. If these assumptions hold true now, when why do we see growth in senior engineering jobs?
